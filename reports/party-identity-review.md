# Party identity review

## 1. Overview

We compared the preserved Oireachtas records of parties and members of the
Dáil and Seanad. The records identify **315 distinct non-Independent party
entries**. This review proposes **33 political-party groupings** as a starting
point for human review. Four records are held out: one unresolved Sinn Féin
entry, two term-specific Business and Professional Group entries, and one
Ceann Comhairle office entry. The proposals
are not final claims that similarly named entries always belong to the same
organisation.

The aim is to help a reviewer decide which parliamentary records can reasonably
be grouped under the same enduring political-party name, and which need more
careful consideration. Dates shown below are the parliamentary periods in
which records were observed; they are not dates when a party began or ended.

## 3. Cases requiring review

### Sinn Féin: three separate treatments

The records use the name Sinn Féin in the 1st–6th Dáils, again in the 16th
Dáil, and in much later Dáil and Seanad periods. This review keeps the 1st–6th
Dáil entries as **historical Sinn Féin**, proposes the later records from the
28th Dáil and 23rd Seanad onward as **modern Sinn Féin**, and leaves the **16th
Dáil entry unresolved**. No continuity within the 1st–6th Dáil is presumed.

Some old Sinn Féin party-date entries do not fit the parliamentary membership
period alongside which they appear. In particular, records for later early
Dáils contain party dates from earlier years. These values have not been
corrected. **Reviewer decision:** keep the 16th Dáil record unresolved, and
decide whether the early entries can be grouped as proposed after the dated
source discrepancies are examined.

### Possible party name changes or related organisations

The source names suggest possible relationships, but do not settle them:

| Records that may be related | What the reviewer should decide |
|---|---|
| Cumann na nGaedheal / Fine Gael | Should these be treated as one continuing party identity, separate identities, or a predecessor/successor relationship? |
| National Progressive Democrats / Progressive Democrats | Are these one organisation under different names, or distinct parties? |
| Democratic Left / Labour Party | Should the records be linked as predecessor/successor, or kept distinct? |
| Anti-Austerity Alliance – People Before Profit / People Before Profit Alliance / People Before Profit–Solidarity | Which records name a party, an alliance, or a combined electoral grouping? Which, if any, belong to one party identity? |
| Workers Party / Sinn Féin the Workers Party | Are these distinct organisations or related identities? Do not merge them solely on the shared name. |
| Sinn Féin / Sinn Féin the Workers Party | Keep distinct unless evidence supports a reviewed relationship. |

The review proposal does not assert that any pair above is identical or a
successor. The reviewer can approve separate identities, combine entries, or
record a relationship without claiming they are the same party.

### Records that may not describe political parties

Most entries are named like parties, but a party list may also contain a
parliamentary office, a group of representatives, or an electoral alliance.
The records include **Ceann Comhairle** (the Dáil Speaker's office) and
**Business and Professional Group**. These have not been counted among the 33
proposed political parties. **Reviewer decision:** confirm they should remain
outside the party identities. The names **100% RDR**, **Republican**,
**Independents 4 Change**, **Workers and Unemployed Action**, and the combined
People Before Profit names also merit a category check; their appearance in a
party list alone does not settle whether each is a registered political party
or an electoral grouping.

### Dates that need checking

Member records include different dated party-affiliation intervals, so the
earliest and latest dates in the inventory are summaries of those observations,
not the life span of a party. Some source intervals end before the related
member's recorded service in that House term, and some intervals have an end
before their start. The CSV preserves the dates and flags these cases; nothing
has been silently repaired. **Reviewer decision:** determine whether these are
source-history errors or reflect a different meaning for the dates.

## 4. Limitations

The preserved Parties files contain five repeated captures of the same current
party list: 55 rows collapse to **11 distinct records**, of which **10 are
non-Independent**. All 10 also appear in the Member records. The preserved
Member files contribute **315 distinct non-Independent term-specific records**;
the combined inventory therefore contains 315, with no Parties-only eligible
record omitted. The five Parties captures do not provide a full historical
register, so historical coverage here comes from the dated party entries in
Member records. The Member files also contain 63 distinct Independent records,
and the Parties files contain one distinct Independent record; these are
excluded from the political-party inventory.

These records show when party affiliations and parliamentary memberships were
reported in the available material. They cannot by themselves establish when a
party was founded or dissolved, or prove that organisations with similar names
across different periods were continuously the same organisation. Review is
still required before any identity is treated as settled.

For the complete record-by-record evidence, source identifiers, all observed
dates, flags and reproducible generation command, see
[`party-identity-inventory.csv`](party-identity-inventory.csv) and
[`tools/party_identity_inventory.py`](../tools/party_identity_inventory.py).
